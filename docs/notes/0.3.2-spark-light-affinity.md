# Lantern 0.3.2 Spark-Light Affinity Regression

> **Applies to:** automatic 3D and Three.js's forced WebGL 2 backend
>
> **Authority:** presentation only; simulation physics, particle state, schema v4, recordings, and replay are unchanged

## Symptom

Near the end of a spark shower, a point light could jump from its dying carrier
to an older surviving spark. The reassignment produced a brief flash at a new
tail position even though no new fire effect had appeared.

Two independent index-based choices caused the artifact. The light budget filled
every newly available spark slot from all currently eligible particles, while
the resident point-light pool applied each frame's assignments by array index.
An expired pulse, projectile, or spark could therefore move several surviving
sources and backfill the opening with an old unlit particle.

## Required invariants

- Particle IDs are observed for the current simulation timeline. Only a
  first-observed ID may receive a new spark lease.
- A leased spark remains bound until its carrier disappears. If an explosion or
  projectile preempts the lease, that old carrier cannot relight later.
- Removing a carrier leaves its logical capacity dark. An already-observed unlit
  spark never backfills the opening; a particle from a genuinely later burst
  may claim it on the frame where that new ID is first observed.
- Disabling dynamic lights or observing a lower simulation tick clears lease and
  observation state.
- A spark with normalized remaining life `life` uses
  `fade = life² × (3 - 2 × life)` and
  `intensity = (4 + 9 × normalizedMaximumSize) × fade`. Maximum brightness is
  unchanged, and the light approaches zero continuously before removal.
- Resident point lights reconcile by assignment key. Every surviving source
  keeps its exact `PointLight` slot; missing keys zero their former slots; new
  keys claim empty slots in deterministic resident order.
- All eight point lights stay visible, non-shadow-casting, and scene-resident.
  `residentLightCount` remains eight while `activeLightCount` follows logical
  assignments.

## Deterministic regression

A single explosion frame with one live pulse and at least eight sparks allocates
the pulse plus seven spark carriers. When the pulse expires, its resident slot
must become dark instead of selecting an eighth particle from the same shower.
From there, active tail lights may only decrease as their carriers disappear,
and no new particle assignment key may appear during that shower's teardown.

Automated tests also cover later-burst insertion into a dark slot, low-life
carrier retention, explosion and projectile preemption, timeline reset,
`dynamicLights=false`, keyed removal and insertion, disabled pools, and
`0 -> 8 -> 0` topology transitions.

## Manual acceptance

Pause and step through one explosion in automatic 3D, then repeat with
`?renderer=3d&backend=webgl`. No light may change carrier during the tail;
brightness should fade instead of popping near removal; active lights should
step downward while all eight residents remain compiled. Rapid overlapping
fireballs must still illuminate genuinely new effects without flashing at old
positions. Canvas2D is unchanged.
