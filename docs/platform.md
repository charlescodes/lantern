# Platform Contract

> **Current through:** Lantern 0.9.0

The engine is browser-first and built with standard JavaScript ES modules, HTML, and CSS. It targets modern desktop browsers, while Node.js is used only for development tooling, dependency management, testing, and the local development server. The core simulation should remain independent of the browser, renderer, and input devices so it can later run in a Web Worker, Node.js server, Electron application, or another host with minimal changes. The prototype runs entirely on the client, but the code preserves a clear boundary between commands, simulation state, and presentation so authoritative multiplayer can be added later without redesigning the engine.

Simulation truth remains a fixed 60 Hz X/Z world expressed in meters, seconds, and kilograms. Commands are consumed only at fixed-tick boundaries. The grid, actor collision, projectile collision, line-of-effect, health, enemy decisions, encounter cadence, and replay state belong to `src/sim`; Canvas2D, Three.js, DOM panels, height, emissive response, lights, and TrueSight concealment consume snapshots without simulation authority.

The current serialization boundary is snapshot/recording schema v9, scenario schema v3, map schema v1, Fireball definition format v1, and performance-report schema v2. Schema-v9 recordings name `obelisk-duel-v1` gameplay and `investigative-wizard-v1` enemy AI, with `enemyCapacity: 64` and `encounterMaximumAlive: 4`. Schema-v8 recordings retain the exact `perceptive-wizard-v1` behavior and scheduling. Schema-v7 recordings retain the exact omniscient `tactical-wizard-v1` profile and historical four-entry enemy pool. Schema-v6 recordings retain `basic-wizard-v1`; schema v5 keeps the versioned Fireball registry with frozen pre-combat behavior; schema-v2 through v4 keep their historical Fireball, particle, and collision profiles. Legacy scenario v2 and map v1 imports have no obelisk and therefore no encounter.

The default scenario owns exactly one invulnerable obelisk. Its solid cell is ordinary simulation occlusion for actors, projectiles, blast rays, AI line-of-sight, and TrueSight, while renderers replace that wall cell with procedural obelisk geometry. Live enemy wizards occupy a 64-entry typed-array pool, while the authored encounter still attempts one spawn on tick 1, repeats every 1,800 ticks, and caps at four alive. Player and enemy casts resolve through the same versioned Fireball registry, but each caster owns independent cooldown and successful-cast sequence state. The default projectile pool is 256 and the particle pool remains 4,096 with bounded drop behavior.

Live visual perception is geometry-only simulation authority. A qualifying player or hostile-Fireball sample requires the center within 12m, clear grid line-of-sight, and either a 120-degree cone around normalized authoritative facing or the 1.5m 360-degree close radius. Walls and the obelisk occlude. Rocks, darkness, lights, TrueSight, particles, renderer state, and presentation height do not. Samples run at 12Hz across five deterministic spawn-sequence lanes; 15 uninterrupted ticks of qualifying player samples confirm the player, while one visible Fireball sample is sufficient because projectile position, velocity, and age are authoritative. Projectile owner identity is never used for exposure or caster identification. Facing turns at no more than 180 degrees per second. Unaware guards store their spawn point and sweep 45 degrees to either side of their base heading over a six-second local-phase cycle.

Confirmed target knowledge and investigation clues are personal. Each mob records last-seen position, velocity, and tick; losing sight stops casting, travels to that point, searches reachable radius-1 through radius-3 cells for exactly eight seconds, then returns to guard and clears memory. A completed destination field must prove a guard unreachable continuously for 12 seconds before the current reachable cell becomes the new guard. A visible hostile Fireball infers its clamped launch point as `position - velocity × age`. Living hostile wizards hear its exact impact point within an inclusive 16m regardless of walls, rocks, darkness, or TrueSight. Unseen damage records only its impact point and starts an immediate local search. Evidence is accepted centrally in descending priority: current direct player sight, visible Fireball trajectory, stored last-seen player position, personal damage, then heard explosion. Newer equal-priority clues redirect; repeated samples and the same effect's later explosion are deduplicated. Anonymous evidence never identifies a caster, advances exposure, or permits firing. This release has one generic actor target, the player; footsteps, surface acoustics, wall attenuation, general sound events, squad sharing, co-op selection, networking, and friendly/critter perception remain outside the authority boundary.

The destination cache owns four pinned actor-target slots and 64 shared goal-cell slots. Every slot retains one completed cost array, while builds serialize through one preallocated Dijkstra workspace in stable slot order under a single 2,048-expansion tick budget. A previous completed array remains available while its replacement builds, but movers use direct wall-sliding until the requested field is current. Approach follows decreasing target cost; withdrawal follows increasing hostile cost; hunt/investigation/search/return follow decreasing goal cost. A preallocated map-cell broadphase supplies deterministic actor, rock, swept-projectile, visual-projectile, dodge, and hearing candidates in historical body-class and pool-index order. Schema v9 rebuilds it before perception, reuses that build for visual Fireball observation and dodge selection, then rebuilds after movement for collision and impact hearing. Schema-v8 scheduling is unchanged. Dynamic rocks and actors remain local obstacles rather than navigation-field inputs.

Tactical choices remain isolated from global simulation RNG and presentation state. Stable enemy spawn sequence and named hash lanes drive guard headings/phases, search transforms/scans, strafe side/timing, and dodge ties. While engaged, v9 retains the 0.7 range control, strafe, intercept, visible-projectile dodge, and health retreat. Casting requires engaged state, no retreat, and a fresh same-tick cone/range/grid-LOS check; remembered or inferred coordinates are never firing targets. Dodge and retreat override movement without clearing perception or investigation state or pausing hunt/search clocks. TrueSight supplies no AI knowledge.

Combat health is symmetric and fixed outside Fireball definition v1: actors have 100 maximum health, direct opponent hits deal 25, and splash damage uses the captured blast radius with linear surface-distance falloff. Team immunity affects health and projectile actor collision only; physical explosion impulse remains neutral. Defeat freezes autonomous gameplay for 90 fixed ticks while reset and Spell Lab actions remain authoritative, then rebuilds the same-seed encounter baseline while preserving the applied spell revision and panel draft.

Presentation must keep Canvas2D and Three.js behavior aligned from the same snapshot data. The Three.js adapter preallocates 64 enemy instances, 64 facing markers, and 65 health-track/fill instances before warmup. The procedural front marker derives from the same normalized facing snapshot in both renderers, shares enemy TrueSight concealment, and adds no light. Health tracks and fills retain their established transparent render ordering; the separately recorded through-wall health-bar defect remains outside 0.8.0. TrueSight hides an enemy, its marker, health bar, and every hostile effect carrier together, but never changes AI knowledge or replay truth. AI View remains a shared read-only overlay: Off/Selected/All semantics and stable selection do not inject commands, and its clearly labeled mob-vision diagnostics intentionally remain visible through TrueSight for developer inspection.

The ordinary browser route boots into full-viewport play mode. One local semicolon gate owns the top controls, authoring windows, instrumentation, coordinate/help chrome, inspection, AI View drawing, and presentation diagnostics. Closing that gate never changes snapshot, command, replay, or recorded particle-collision configuration; it only suppresses developer presentation and disables developer keyboard shortcuts. Spell Lab starts parked behind its toolbox launcher, and collapsing it removes the panel from the arena while retaining its local draft state.

In play mode, both presentation cameras focus every rendered frame on the same locally interpolated X/Z pose used to draw the player. There is no spring, dead zone, prediction, or network buffering. A stationary viewport pointer is reprojected after camera movement so movement targets, casts, cursor markers, and inspection stay aligned. Wheel zoom preserves the player-centered target and MMB cannot detach the play camera. Edit mode inherits the last play view, then retains cursor-anchored zoom, world-meter MMB panning, and explicit Focus/F recentering until it returns to play.
